# Writing and locking the script

The script is the contract. It is written, shown and approved before a sandbox is opened, a voice is generated or a render is quoted.

## Length from duration
About 2.5 spoken words per second in Spanish and English.
| Duration | Words |
|---|---|
| 20 s | 50 |
| 30 s | 75 |
| 45 s | 110 |
| 60 s | 150 |
| 90 s | 225 |
Portuguese runs a little longer: trim 10 percent. Count the words of the spoken lines only, not the on-screen text.

## Structure
1. Hook (up to 3 seconds): a question or a surprising fact that matches the video's promise.
2. Beats (3 to 5): one idea each, with one visual each.
3. Recap: one line.
4. Call to action: one line (follow, try, read more).

## The script format to show the user
One row per scene:
| # | Seconds | Voice line | On screen | Visual |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–3 | “¿Sabías que tu plata puede crecer sola?” | ¿Plata que crece sola? | a coin splitting in two |

Keep the voice line and the on-screen words as separate columns: they are not the same text.

## Writing for the ear
- Short sentences, one idea per sentence, punctuation where a person would pause.
- Numbers spelled the way they should sound; the digits go on screen.
- No jargon without a definition, one definition per beat at most.
- Exact figures for anything shown on screen. For compound interest, give the series (1000, 1100, 1210, 1331), not "it grows".
- Facts the user can verify. Do not invent statistics, sources or quotes. When a figure is uncertain, ask or leave it out.

## Locking
- Show the script and ask for approval: Approve · Change something. Only then continue.
- After approval, change a line only when the user asks. If a voice line turns out too long for its scene, shorten the line and tell the user; never speed up the audio.
- A line changed after approval is shown again before the render.

## Per-line limits
- A voice line fits its scene: a line that does not fit is shortened, not rushed.
- At most 3 attempts per voice line. After the third, shorten the line or ask the user which words to cut.
- Each attempt is checked against the scene's length before it is placed.

## Language
The voice and the on-screen words use the language the user chose, with the right accents and punctuation. A bilingual video is two renders: do the second one only when the user asks.
