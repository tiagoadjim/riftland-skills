# Words on a thumbnail

The words are the part that fails most often: too many, too small, misspelled or hidden. Decide them before the brief.

## How many
- 2 to 4 words. One short line. Never a sentence.
- No words at all is a valid choice when the picture tells the story.
- Never copy the video's title: the title is already next to the thumbnail. Add a second hook, a number or an emotion.

## What to write
- A claim in few words: "SE ROMPIÓ", "NADIE LO SABE", "30 DÍAS".
- A question only if it is under 4 words.
- In the language of the video. Keep accents and the ñ.
- Capitals read best at small size; use sentence case only for calm, editorial looks.

## Spelling
- Put the words in quotes in the request, exactly as they must appear.
- After the result, read the image with `media_view` and compare letter by letter. A wrong or missing letter is a redo.
- If a word fails twice, change the word to a shorter one before the third try (the third attempt must change something).

## Legible at small size
- Very large: the words take a third of the image or more.
- High contrast with what is behind: light words on dark, or dark on a bright flat area, with an outline or a solid block if needed.
- One typeface style, heavy and simple.
- Keep the bottom-right corner free: the video's duration badge covers it on most surfaces.
- Keep the words away from the edges, with a margin of a few percent.

## The check
Look at the result and ask three things:
1. Can the words be read if the image were the size of a thumbnail in a list?
2. Is each word spelled right?
3. Does the face or the object stay visible with the words in place?
If any answer is no, tweak with `change_of` (one change) before anything else.

## What not to do
- No emojis or symbols as words.
- No long text, subtitles or watermarks.
- No small print, dates or links.
