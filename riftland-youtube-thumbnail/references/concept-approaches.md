# Composition approaches

Pick 3 to 5 approaches that fit the video, each different from the others. Each line below gives the pattern, when it works and the one risk. Write the concept to the user as: a name, the picture in one sentence, the words.

## The approaches
1. Reaction close-up. A large face on one third, a clear emotion, the thing it reacts to on the other side. Works for tests, reviews and surprises. Risk: the emotion looks fake if the photo is neutral.
2. Split verdict. The frame divided in two: before and after, or this against that, with the face or the object on each side. Works for comparisons and transformations. Risk: both halves need equal weight or the story is unclear.
3. Giant object. One object far larger than it should be, with the person small next to it. Works for products, gadgets and places. Risk: scale confuses when the object is unrecognizable.
4. Silhouette mystery. A dark figure or shape with one bright detail and a question as the words. Works for stories and reveals. Risk: needs strong contrast; a busy background kills it.
5. Big number. A numeral or a short word as the hero, the face small in a corner. Works for lists, rankings, days and money. Risk: the number must be true to the video.
6. Cutaway. A cross-section or a see-through view of the thing the video explains. Works for how things work. Risk: too much detail unreadable at small size.
7. Two-choice duel. Two options face to face with the person between or above them. Works for picks and polls. Risk: the options must be visually distinct.
8. Over-the-shoulder reveal. The viewer looks past the person at a surprising scene. Works for places, events and discoveries. Risk: the scene must be bright enough to read.
9. Evidence flat lay. Objects arranged from above as if on a desk, with a handwritten word. Works for tutorials, tools and collections. Risk: looks flat if the arrangement has no focal point.
10. Clean statement. One subject on a plain color with three words. Works for calm, premium or educational channels. Risk: boring if the subject has no personality.

## Choosing
- Match the approach to the video's promise: a transformation wants the split verdict; a ranking wants the big number.
- Make the set varied in two ways at least: the layout (where the face goes) and the color family.
- Always include one clean, low-risk concept; the user often picks it.

## Writing the line for the user
Use this shape, one line each: "1 · Reacción: tu cara grande a la derecha, el celular roto a la izquierda, texto SE ROMPIÓ." Add the approach name in the user's language. Do not describe light or lenses at this stage.

## When every concept starts to look alike
- Change the approach, not the adjectives.
- Move the face: left, right, center, small in a corner, absent.
- Change the color family completely for at least one concept.
- Replace the face with an object for one concept.
